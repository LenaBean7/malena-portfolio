方法スタイル

How I work

Five things I bring to a team — tap each to read more.

01

Develop Detail Orientation

Tap to read moreTap to collapse

I strongly believe in the importance of practicing and continually honing attention to detail. In the aerospace industry, errors can come at a high cost to stakeholders in terms of finances, time, and, most importantly, safety. For this reason, I intentionally seek out opportunities to practically improve my methods, observation skills, and habits. By doing so, I am able to fine-tune the quality of my work while simultaneously increasing my efficiency, adaptability, and precision.

02

Understand and Derive Procedure

Tap to read moreTap to collapse

Building on the necessity for strong attention-to-detail habits, the next step is developing consolidated procedures that can be clearly understood and consistently executed by another individual. This is where my experience in tool development is best demonstrated. When developing a digital or procedural tool, my thought process follows a consistent process: first, establish an optimized and meticulous practice; second, identify recurring steps; and finally, consolidate or automate those steps. I use this approach to improve efficiency without ever compromising attention to detail or quality.

This mindset has become a framework for my problem-solving process and has been continually refined through my experience working on multiple shop floors. It allows me to identify inefficiencies, develop practical solutions, and create tools and procedures that make complex tasks more consistent, efficient, and repeatable.

03

Practice Intentional, Optimized Communication

Tap to read moreTap to collapse

With a diverse background as both a technician and a pilot, I am particularly attentive to the communication challenges that can arise between engineers and technical professionals. I believe engineers have a responsibility to seek out an understanding of technical practices so that they can communicate information in terms that translate effectively into practical implementation. I apply this principle in my own work by consistently engaging with the technical aspects of a project, asking questions, and challenging my own assumptions about what is — and is not — supported by traceable logic.

I view my role as an engineer foremost as one of facilitating the work of technicians, whether they are maintainers or pilots in command. By understanding their needs and communicating clearly, engineers can help create an environment in which technical professionals can work efficiently and safely, ultimately producing the highest-quality product possible.

04

Understand Through Hands-On Implementation

Tap to read moreTap to collapse

Touching on my technical background, these skills have allowed me to better visualize and understand nearly every project I have worked on. When writing build plans, I am able to put myself in the technician's position and mentally walk through the build, identifying potential challenges before they arise. When working with flight simulations and automation, my experience as a pilot in command provides invaluable insight into control logic, operational considerations, and scenario analysis. Each of these experiences builds upon the others, allowing me to approach engineering problems from multiple perspectives and contribute as a more well-rounded team member.

05

Commit to Continuous Improvement

Tap to read moreTap to collapse

My most valuable asset is my commitment to continuous improvement. This mindset has shaped both my personal and professional goals and has been one of the most effective tools in helping me achieve them. I believe that the direction in which you choose to look ultimately influences the direction in which you move. In every engineering role I have undertaken, I have found that setting goals early allows me to intentionally seek out learning opportunities and, more importantly, understand how to apply that knowledge in future roles.

Whether the skill involves communication, procedural development, technical expertise, or leadership, I consistently look for ways to improve. I view every experience as an opportunity to refine my abilities, expand my perspective, and become a more capable engineer.

Attitude control flight software
